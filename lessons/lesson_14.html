Questions are how a learner gets unstuck. Once you can ask "what is this?", "how much?" and "where is it?", every conversation becomes a chance to learn rather than a test you might fail.

1. The twelve question words

KapampanganEnglishExample
nanuwhatNanu ini? — What is this?
ninuwhoNinu i Jennifer? — Who is Jennifer?
nukarinwhereNukarin ya i Henry? — Where is Henry?
kapilanwhenKapilan ya ing pista? — When is the fiesta?
obakitwhyObakit ati ka keni? — Why are you here?
kaninuwhose, to whomKaninung kalikubak ini? — Whose dandruff is this?
makananuhowMakananu iti gawan? — How is this done?
pilanhow manyPilan a kapaya? — How many papayas?
magkanuhow much (price)Magkanu ya ini? — How much is this?
nuantito what degreeNuanti ka kalagu? — How beautiful you are!

komustahow is…?Komusta ka? — How are you?

⚠️ obakit, not bakit

Tagalog speakers reach for bakit. Kapampangan says obakit. This is one of the quickest ways to mark yourself as speaking Kapampangan rather than Tagalog with local vocabulary sprinkled in.

2. The core five

If you learn five today, learn these. They will carry you through almost any practical situation.

Nanu ini? — What is this?
The single most productive sentence for a beginner. Point, ask, learn a word.

Nukarin ya ing ___? — Where is the ___?
Nukarin ya ing drayber? — Where is the driver?

Magkanu ya ini? — How much is this?
The market sentence. Lesson 18 builds a whole transaction around it.

Nanu ya ing buri mu? — What do you want?
And its mirror, Nanu ya ing gagawan mu? — What are you doing?

Komusta ka? — How are you?
From lesson 2, still doing more work than any other phrase you know.

Notice how often ya appears. The agreement rule from lesson 4 applies to questions exactly as it does to statements.

3. Pairs English collapses

pilan vs magkanu

English says "how much" for both quantity and price. Kapampangan does not.

pilancounts thingsPilan a kapaya? — How many papayas?
magkanuasks a priceMagkanu ya ing tinape? — How much is the bread?

makananu vs nuanti

Both translate as "how", but they ask different things.

makananuby what methodMakananu meng gewa ita? — How did you do that?
nuantito what degreeNuanti ya karagul? — How big is it?

nukarin — only for things, not actions

Nukarin asks where a person or object is. It is not used to ask where an action takes place.

Nukarin ya ing drayber? ✔ — Where is the driver?

4. Yes-or-no questions

The simplest yes-or-no question is a statement with rising intonation. But Kapampangan also has a particle: wari.

Malyari ku waring lumwal? — May I go out?

Mangabayatan ka? — Is it heavy?

Akakit me? — Do you see him?

Wari is roughly Tagalog's ba, though not an exact match. Two other particles do related work:

ParticleEffect
warimarks a question
kaya"I wonder", "how about" — Tsa kaya? "How about tea?"
yata / ata"probably, it seems" — softens a claim

💡 Questions and fused pronouns

Remember from lesson 5 that speakers tend to avoid fusing pronouns in questions. So you will hear Akakit me? but also plenty of unfused forms in interrogatives where a statement would fuse them.

5. Answering

Answers to obakit generally lead with a cause word:

KapampanganEnglish
kasibecause
uli ningbecause of

— Obakit ati ka keni? Why are you here?
— Kasi bisa kung mangan. Because I want to eat.

And when you cannot answer, lesson 13 already gave you the sentence: E ku balu.

6. Practice

Exercise 1: Which question word?

  • "What is this?" → "___ ini?" (Answer: nanu)
  • "Who is Jennifer?" → "___ i Jennifer?" (Answer: ninu)
  • "Where is Henry?" → "___ ya i Henry?" (Answer: nukarin)
  • "When is the fiesta?" → "___ ya ing pista?" (Answer: kapilan)
  • "Why are you here?" → "___ ati ka keni?" (Answer: obakit)

Exercise 2: pilan or magkanu?

  • Asking the price of bread: ___ (Answer: magkanu)
  • Asking how many papayas: ___ (Answer: pilan)
  • Asking someone's age: ___ (Answer: pilan)

Exercise 3: makananu or nuanti?

  • "How did you do that?" → "___ meng gewa ita?" (Answer: makananu)
  • "How big is it?" → "___ ya karagul?" (Answer: nuanti)

Exercise 4: Not bakit

  • The Kapampangan word for "why" is ___ (Answer: obakit)
  • The particle that marks a yes-or-no question is ___ (Answer: wari)
  • "because" → ___ (Answer: kasi)

Exercise 5: Don't drop the pronoun

  • "Where is the driver?" → "Nukarin ___ ing drayber?" (Answer: ya)
  • "Who are those men?" → "Ninu ___ reng lalaki?" (Answer: la)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do question words always come first? Overwhelmingly yes — the question word takes the front of the sentence, and the pronoun follows immediately.

What if I use bakit by mistake? You will be understood everywhere in Pampanga, because everyone speaks Tagalog too. But you will be understood as a Tagalog speaker, which rather defeats the purpose.

Is nuanti really common? Less so than the others, but Nuanti ka kalagu? is a well-known compliment and worth recognising.

Why This Lesson Matters

Every other lesson in this course teaches you to say things. This one teaches you to find out things — which means it is the lesson that keeps working after the course ends. Nanu ini? is a vocabulary-acquisition engine you can carry into any conversation in Pampanga.
