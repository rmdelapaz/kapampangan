English has one set of pronouns with a small case wrinkle: I / me, he / him. Kapampangan has three full sets, and which one you use depends on the job the pronoun is doing in the sentence — exactly the same logic as the markers in lesson 3.

This lesson is dense. Do not try to memorise all three tables today. Learn the singular forms cold, get the shape of the rest, and come back.

1. Three sets, not one

graph TD P[A Kapampangan pronoun] --> Q{What is its job?} Q -->|"the one the sentence is about"| A["SUBJECT set
yaku, ika, iya — ku, ka, ya"] Q -->|"the doer, backgrounded — or a possessor"| B["DOER set
ku, mu, na, mi, tamu, yu, da"] Q -->|"to / for / at someone"| C["OBLIQUE set
kaku, keka, keya, karela"]

The subject set pairs with the marker ing. The doer set pairs with ning. The oblique set pairs with king. If you keep that alignment in mind, the three tables stop being arbitrary lists.

2. The subject set

These come in two shapes. The full forms stand alone and carry emphasis. The short forms lean on the preceding word and are what you use most of the time.

MeaningFull formShort form
Iyaku, akuku
you (one person)ikaka
he, she, itiya, yaya
we two (you and I)ikatakata, ta
we, including youikatamu, itamukatamu, tamu
we, excluding youikamikami, ke
you (plural)ikayu, ikokayu, ko
theyilala

Mamasa la. — They are reading.

Tatakut kami. — We are afraid.

Muli ku. — I will go home.

Dintang ya. — He (or she) arrived.

Notice the pattern: verb first, pronoun immediately after. The short pronoun is almost a suffix on the verb.

3. The doer set

This set does two jobs that English treats as unrelated: it marks a backgrounded doer, and it marks possession.

MeaningForm
by me / myku
by you / yourmu
by him, her / his, herna
by us two / ourta
by us incl. you / ourtamu
by us excl. you / ourmi
by you (pl.) / youryu
by them / theirda, ra

⚠️ ku appears in both sets

Yes, ku means both "I" (subject set) and "my / by me" (doer set). This is not a typo and it is not a trap — the position in the sentence tells you which is which. After a verb it is the subject; after a noun it is possessive.

Muli ku = "I will go home."
ing bale ku = "my house."

Possessive pronouns follow the noun they modify:

ing bale ku — my house

ing lagyu mu — your name

ing salamin mu — your glasses

4. Three ways to say "we"

English "we" is ambiguous: "we're going to dinner" may or may not include the person you are speaking to. Kapampangan refuses that ambiguity and gives you three pronouns.

PronounWho it coversUse it when…
ikata / kataexactly you and me — two peoplejust the pair of you
ikatamu / katamume, you, and othersyou are included
ikami / kamime and others, not youyou are excluded

Ala katang nasi. — We two have no rice.

Ala tamung nasi. — We (including you) have no rice.

Ala keng nasi. — We (not including you) have no rice.

🏛️ This is a social choice, not just grammar

Choosing ikatamu over ikami literally includes your listener in the group. Using the wrong one can quietly exclude someone, or presumptuously include them. Speakers notice. When you invite someone to eat, Mangan tana! uses the inclusive form on purpose.

5. To me, to you, to them

The oblique set is what follows king-type meanings: to, for, at.

MeaningForm
to mekaku, kanaku
to youkeka
to him, to herkeya, kaya
to us (excl.)kekami
to us (incl.)kekatamu
to you (pl.)kekayu
to themkarela

Sabian me kaku. — Tell it to me.

Ninu ya ing minaus keka? — Who called you?

The oblique forms can also replace a possessive — but then they come before the noun rather than after it, and the effect is emphatic:

ing bale ku — my house

ing kakung bale — my house (emphasis on whose)

ing kanakung bale — same, fuller form

6. The rule you cannot skip

Kapampangan requires the pronoun even when the noun is already in the sentence.

This is the single most reliable way to spot a learner who came from Tagalog. Tagalog drops the pronoun when the noun is present. Kapampangan does not.

✔ Correct✘ WrongMeaning
Dintang ya i ErningDintang i ErningErnie arrived
Mamasa la ri Maria at JuanMamasa ri Maria at JuanMaria and Juan are reading
Silatanan na kang JoséSilatanan kang JoséHe wrote to José

Think of the pronoun as grammatical agreement, the way English forces "he walks" rather than "he walk". It is not redundant. It is required.

🌱 If this feels like a lot

It is a lot. Three pronoun sets plus an agreement rule is genuinely the hardest part of beginner Kapampangan, and you are meeting it in lesson 4 rather than lesson 14 because everything else depends on it.

You are not expected to produce all of this fluently today. Aim to recognise the forms now; production follows recognition by a long way, and that gap is normal rather than a sign you are failing.

7. Practice

Exercise 1: Short subject pronouns

  • "I will go home." → "Muli ___." (Answer: ku)
  • "They are reading." → "Mamasa ___." (Answer: la)
  • "He arrived." → "Dintang ___." (Answer: ya)
  • "We (excluding you) are afraid." → "Tatakut ___." (Answer: kami)

Exercise 2: Possession

  • "my house" → "ing bale ___" (Answer: ku)
  • "your name" → "ing lagyu ___" (Answer: mu)
  • "his dog" → "ing asu ___" (Answer: na)
  • "their house" → "ing bale ___" (Answer: da|ra)

Exercise 3: Which "we"?

Write kata, tamu or kami.

  • Just you and me, nobody else: ___ (Answer: kata)
  • Me and my family, and you are not part of it: ___ (Answer: kami)
  • All of us here, you included: ___ (Answer: tamu)

Exercise 4: Add the obligatory pronoun

  • "Ernie arrived." → "Dintang ___ i Erning." (Answer: ya)
  • "Where is Henry?" → "Nukarin ___ i Henry?" (Answer: ya)
  • "Who are those men?" → "Ninu ___ reng lalaki?" (Answer: la)

Exercise 5: To whom?

  • "Tell it to me." → "Sabian me ___." (Answer: kaku|kanaku)
  • "Who called you?" → "Ninu ya ing minaus ___?" (Answer: keka)
  • "to them" → ___ (Answer: karela)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why does ku mean two different things? Historically they are related, and in practice the position disambiguates completely. After a verb: "I". After a noun: "my". You will stop noticing the overlap within a week.

Do I really need three words for "we"? You need to understand all three immediately, because you will hear them constantly. For producing speech, start with tamu (inclusive) and kami (exclusive); kata can wait.

What happens when two pronouns land next to each other? They usually fuse into a single syllable. That is lesson 5, and it is the most Kapampangan thing in the entire language.

Why This Lesson Matters

Almost every sentence you will ever say in Kapampangan contains at least one pronoun from these tables, and the obligatory-agreement rule means you can't quietly avoid them the way a beginner can in English. This is the lesson that earns the rest of the course.
