Two topics that look unrelated belong together for a practical reason: both are things you need to discuss when you are not at your best, and both are governed by the same handy word, panaun — which covers weather, season and time all at once.

1. The body

KapampanganEnglish
katawanbody
buntukhead
mataeye
arungnose
asbukmouth
gamathand
bitisfoot, leg
pusuheart

Several of these differ from Tagalog in ways you cannot guess — buntuk (Tagalog ulo), arung (ilong), asbuk (bibig), gamat (kamay). Others, like mata, are shared.

2. Saying what hurts

The frame is masakit ya ing + [body part] + [possessive].

Masakit ya ing buntuk ku.
My head hurts.

Masakit ya ing atian ku. — My stomach hurts.

Masakit ya ing bitis ku. — My leg hurts.

Everything in that frame is familiar: masakit is a ma- adjective from lesson 15, ya is the obligatory pronoun from lesson 4, ing is the marker from lesson 3, and ku is the possessive from lesson 4. One new pattern, five old pieces.

KapampanganEnglish
sakitillness; pain
masakitit hurts; painful
maglagnatto have a fever

And lesson 10's ati and ala do good service here:

Ati kung sakit. — I have an illness. / I'm unwell.

Ala kung sakit. — I'm not ill.

3. Getting help

KapampanganEnglish
doktordoctor
ospitalhospital
gamutmedicine

Nukarin ya ing ospital? — Where is the hospital?

Kailangan kung doktor. — I need a doctor.

Ala lu ring doktor keni. — The doctors are no longer here.

⚠️ Learn these before you need them

Health vocabulary is the one category where studying in advance genuinely matters. You will not be in a state to look words up when you need them, and in a rural clinic the person helping you may be more comfortable in Kapampangan than in English.

4. Weather

KapampanganEnglish
panaunweather; season; time
uranrain
aldosun; day
anginwind
bagyutyphoon
mapalihot
marimlacold

Mapali ya ngeni. — It's hot now.

Ati yang uran. — There's rain.

🏛️ Two seasons, not four

The Philippines has a dry season and a wet season, not a four-season year. Panaun covering weather, season and time is a neat fit for a climate where the season simply is the weather.

Bagyu is not abstract vocabulary in Central Luzon. Pampanga sits in the typhoon belt and much of it is low-lying floodplain; the word comes up.

5. Days of the week

KapampanganEnglish
dominggoSunday; also "week"
lunesMonday
martesTuesday
mierkulesWednesday
huebesThursday
biernesFriday
sabaduSaturday

These are Spanish borrowings, as they are in Tagalog and most Philippine languages — the seven-day week arrived with the calendar that named it. Months are borrowed the same way (enero, pebrero, marso…).

Combine with lesson 8's time words: napun (yesterday), ngeni (now), bukas (tomorrow), bulan (month), banua (year).

Kapilan ya ing pista? — When is the fiesta?

Dominggo ya. — It's Sunday.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why are the days Spanish? Because the seven-day week itself arrived with Spanish colonisation. Pre-colonial Philippine calendars were organised differently, so there were no native words to keep.

Is masakit "painful" or "difficult"? Both — and context separates them. Masakit ya ing buntuk ku is physical; masakit of a task means hard.

Should I worry about typhoon vocabulary? If you spend a rainy season in Central Luzon, yes. Bagyu, uran and angin are practical words there, not decorative ones.

Why This Lesson Matters

This is the lesson you hope not to need and will be glad to have. It is also the clearest demonstration of how far you have come: the sentence frame for "my head hurts" required no new grammar at all, only a new noun.
