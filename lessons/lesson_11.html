Welcome to A2. From here on, you are building sentences rather than collecting phrases — and that starts with the verb.

Kapampangan verbs are genuinely complex, and a full account is beyond this course. What follows is the working core: enough to say what you did, what you are doing, and what you will do.

1. Aspect, not tense

English verbs mark tense — where an event sits on a timeline. Kapampangan verbs mark aspect — whether the event has started, and whether it has finished.

The practical difference is small most of the time — "not started" usually means future, "finished" usually means past. But the framing matters, because time words like napun, ngeni and bukas (lesson 8) carry more of the load than they would in English.

2. The -um- pattern

-um- is an infix — it goes inside the word, after the first consonant.

AspectFormHow it's builtMeaning
contemplativesumulats + um + ulatwill write
progressivesusulatreduplicate the first syllableis writing
completedsinulats + in + ulatwrote

Sumulat yang poesia ing lalaki. — The boy will write a poem.

Sinali yang nasi. — He bought rice.

The progressive works by reduplication: copy the first consonant and vowel of the root and put it in front. sulat → su-sulat. This is a recurring device across the whole verb system.

3. The mag- pattern

mag- is a prefix, and its completed form is the thing to memorise: mag- becomes meg-.

AspectPrefixExample (lutu — cook)Meaning
contemplativemag-maglutuwill cook
progressivemag- + reduplicationmaglulutuis cooking
completedmeg- (or mig-)meglutucooked

💡 a → e is the signal of completion

Watch that vowel. mag- → meg- is the same change you will see in maN- → meN- below, and in the irregular verbs at the end of this lesson (gawa → gewa, mate → mete).

If a Kapampangan verb has an unexpected e where you expected an a, it is probably completed aspect. That one heuristic will let you parse an enormous amount of speech.

4. The maN- pattern

maN- behaves the same way, becoming meN-. The capital N stands for a nasal that assimilates to the following sound.

AspectFormMeaning
contemplativemanganwill eat
progressivemamanganis eating
completedmenganate

Mangan la keta. — They will eat over there.

Mamangan la ring babi? — They eat pigs too?

Mamasa la. — They are reading.

You met mangan and mamangan in lesson 9 without explanation. Now they have one.

5. The trap for Tagalog speakers

⚠️ susulat does not mean what you think

FormIn KapampanganIn Tagalog
susulatis writingwill write
sumulatwill writewrote
sinulatwrotewrote

The same three written forms exist in both languages and two of the three mean something different. A Tagalog speaker reading Susulat ku as "I will write" has it wrong — it is "I am writing."

This is the clearest possible evidence that Kapampangan is not a dialect of Tagalog. They are separate languages in separate branches, and their shared vocabulary makes the divergences more dangerous, not less.

6. Verbs that change their vowel

A group of common verbs mark completion by changing a vowel rather than adding an affix. These are high-frequency, so learn them as pairs.

BaseCompletedMeaning
gawagewado, make → did, made
matemetedie → died
saliselibuy → bought it
datangdintangarrive → arrived
lukaslikastake off → took off

Mete ya ing sese ku napun. — My pet died yesterday.

Makananu meng gewa ita? — How did you do that?

E ke seli. — I did not buy it.

Dintang ya i Erning. — Ernie arrived.

Other everyday verbs worth having now:

KapampanganEnglish
mulito go home
umeto come, to go
lumwalto go out
tudtudto sleep

Muli ku. — I will go home.

Ali ku mipapatudtud. — I can't sleep.

🌱 On verb tables

Nobody learns a Philippine verb system by memorising tables. What actually happens is that you meet mengan in a sentence thirty times and one day it simply means "ate" without you deriving it.

So use this lesson as a map, not a test. The one thing genuinely worth memorising today is the a→e completion signal.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

How do I know which prefix a verb takes? Largely you don't — you learn it with the verb, the way you learn German noun genders. Some verbs even belong to unpredictable classes, and this is openly acknowledged as one of the hardest things about Kapampangan for speakers of other Philippine languages.

Is there a future tense? Not as such. The contemplative aspect covers it, and time words do the rest.

What about the other affixes I've seen? Kapampangan has a much larger affix inventory than this — i-, -an, ipaN-, ka- and more. Lesson 12 introduces two of them; the rest is B1 material.

Why This Lesson Matters

Until now you have been saying things about the world — names, family, prices, food. Verbs let you say what happens in it. Everything in the remaining lessons — negation, questions, requests, shopping — is built on the forms here.
