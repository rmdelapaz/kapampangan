English points two ways: this and that. Tagalog points three: ito, iyan, iyon. Kapampangan points four, and it also gives every demonstrative a distinct plural — something most Philippine languages do not bother with.

1. Four ways to point

WordDistanceEnglish
itinear me, not near youthis (here by me)
ininear both of usthis (here by us)
itanear youthat (there by you)
iyanaway from us boththat over there, yonder

⚠️ Sources disagree about this table

This course follows Forman's Kapampangan Grammar Notes (1971), which sets the four out as above, organised by whether the thing is near the speaker, near both, near the addressee, or near neither.

Other descriptions — including the English Wikipedia article — order them differently, putting ini nearest the speaker and ita furthest away. That is close to the reverse of Forman on two of the four.

Kapampangan deictics vary by locality, and Forman himself notes that the variation between keta, ken and kyan "is not yet well understood." If the speakers around you use these differently, follow them, not this page. The four-way contrast itself is not in doubt; only which form sits in which slot.

Nanu ini? — What is this?

Ninu ya ing lalaking ita? — Who is that man over there?

Ninu ya ing anak a ian? — Who is that child (near you)?

Buri ne ita. — She likes that.

💡 Pointing is relational

Kapampangan demonstratives are not about metres. They are about whose space the thing is in — mine, ours, yours, or nobody's. Choosing iyan rather than ini quietly acknowledges that the object belongs to your listener's side of the conversation.

2. ini or iti?

Both translate as "this", and this is the pair learners ask about most. The distinction is concrete versus abstract.

 iniiti
Used foralways concrete — things you can point atusually abstract, but can be concrete too
Exampleining libru — this bookiting musika — this music
Exampleini ing asu nang Juan — this is Juan's dogiti ing gagawan mi — this is what we do

A practical rule of thumb: if you could physically put your finger on it, use ini. If it is an idea, a plan, a situation or a piece of music, reach for iti.

3. Plural demonstratives

Each demonstrative has its own plural, with the usual free d-/r- variation.

SingularPluralEnglish
itideti / retithese (by me)
inideni / renithese (by us both)
itadeta / retathose (by you)
iyanden / renthose over there

Mangabanglu la rening sampaga. — These flowers smell nice.

Maniaman la ren. — Those are delicious.

Note the obligatory la in both — the agreement rule from lesson 4 applies here exactly as it does everywhere else.

4. keni, keti, ken, keta

Each demonstrative has a matching location word. These are what you use to say where something is.

Location wordMatchesEnglish
ketiitihere (where I am)
keniinihere (where we both are)
keta / ken / kyanitathere (where you are)
kariniyanover there, away from us both

Ati ku keti. — I am here.

Ume ka keni. / Me keni. — Come here.

Mangan la keta. — They will eat over there.

Obakit ati ka keni? — Why are you here?

💡 Two words for "here"

Both keti and keni come out as "here" in English. The choice encodes whether your listener is inside the space you mean.

On Forman's arrangement, two people standing in the same country would call that country keni — here, where we both are — while each would call their own home town keti, here on my side.

Descriptions that order the deictics the other way swap those two. The distinction is solid; which label carries it is what varies.

Notice Me keni in the examples. That me is a fused pronoun from lesson 5 doing duty in a command — you will meet it again in lesson 17.

5. The irregular row

The iyan row does not follow the pattern the other three do. It is worth flagging so the irregularity does not look like a mistake when you meet it.

You might expectActual form
*deyanden / ren
*keyankarin

And the "near you" locative has three competing shapes — keta, ken and kyan — which Forman records without being able to state the rule that separates them. If that feels unsatisfying, it is: this is a corner of the language that has not been fully described.

There is also a set of presentative forms — oini, oreni, oita and so on — used for "here it is!" and "there it is!":

Oita ya pala ing salamin mu! — So that's where your glasses are!

Oreni la reng aduang regalo para keka. — Here are the two gifts for you.

You do not need to produce these yet. Recognise them and move on.

6. Practice

Exercise 1: Choose the demonstrative

  • A book in your own hand, not near your listener: ___ (Answer: iti)
  • A phone in your friend's hand: ___ (Answer: ita)
  • A house on the far hill, away from you both: ___ (Answer: iyan|ian)
  • The table the two of you are sitting at: ___ (Answer: ini)

Exercise 2: Make it plural

  • ini → ___ (Answer: deni|reni)
  • iti → ___ (Answer: deti|reti)
  • iyan → ___ (Answer: den|ren)
  • ita → ___ (Answer: deta|reta)

Exercise 3: Here and there

  • "I am here (where we both are)." → "Ati ku ___." (Answer: keni)
  • "Come here (to my side)." → "Ume ka ___." (Answer: keti)
  • "They will eat over there, away from us both." → "Mangan la ___." (Answer: karin)
  • "There, where you are" → ___ (Answer: keta|ken|kyan)

Exercise 4: Concrete or abstract

Write ini or iti.

  • ___ ing libru (this book) (Answer: ini)
  • ___ ing gagawan mi (this is what we do) (Answer: iti)

Exercise 5: Don't forget agreement

  • "These flowers smell nice." → "Mangabanglu ___ rening sampaga." (Answer: la)
  • "Those are delicious." → "Maniaman ___ ren." (Answer: la)
  • "What is this?" → "Nanu ___?" (Answer: ini)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Four seems excessive. Can I get by with two? For producing speech, yes — ini and ita will carry you a long way. For understanding, no: you will hear all four constantly and the distinctions are real.

Is iyan spelled ian? You will see both. The y is a glide, exactly as in abak/yabak from lesson 2.

Why does Kapampangan have plural demonstratives when Tagalog doesn't? Different languages draw the line in different places. Kapampangan marks number on demonstratives and markers; Tagalog marks it with a separate word, mga. Neither is more logical — they are just different solutions.

Why This Lesson Matters

Pointing at things is how beginners survive. When your vocabulary runs out, a demonstrative plus a gesture plus Nanu ini? will get you the word you need from whoever you are talking to — which is, in practice, how most of your Kapampangan vocabulary will eventually be learned.
