This is the lesson that lesson 3 kept pointing at. Markers and verb affixes are not two separate systems you have to learn — they are one system, and this lesson is where the halves meet.

1. What focus actually is

Every Kapampangan sentence picks one noun to be the one the sentence is about. That noun gets the marker ing. And the verb carries an affix that announces which role that noun is playing.

graph TD S["You decide what the sentence is ABOUT"] --> V["The VERB takes an affix announcing the role"] V --> M["The MARKERS rearrange to match"] M --> A["ing goes on the focused noun"] M --> B["ning goes on the doer, if the doer is not focused"]

English does something vaguely similar with the passive voice — "the boy wrote the poem" versus "the poem was written by the boy". But English has two options and uses the passive sparingly. Kapampangan has five and uses them constantly.

⚠️ Focus is not voice

It is tempting to file object focus under "passive". Resist it. An object-focus Kapampangan sentence is completely ordinary and carries no sense of formality or evasion. Translating it into an English passive usually makes it sound stilted when the original is not.

2. Actor focus

The sentence is about the doer. The affixes are the ones you met in lesson 11: -um-, mag-, maN-.

Sumulat yang poesia ing lalaki.
The boy will write a poem.

Pull it apart:

PieceJob
S-um-ulatthe verb, marked for actor focus
yathe obligatory pronoun (lesson 4)
-ng poesiathe poem — not in focus
ing lalakithe boy — in focus

3. Object focus

The sentence is about the thing acted on, or about the person receiving the action. The main affixes are i- and -an.

Isulat ne ning lalaki ing poesia.
The boy will write the poem.

Same event, different framing. Now:

PieceJob
I-sulatthe verb, marked for object focus
nethe fused pronoun — na + ya (lesson 5)
ning lalakithe boy — still the doer, but backgrounded
ing poesiathe poem — now in focus

And with -an, which puts the recipient in focus:

Silatanan ke. — I wrote to him.

Silatanan na ku. — He wrote to me.

Silatanan na kang José. — He wrote to José.

💡 Lesson 5 comes home

Look at Isulat ne and Silatanan ke. Object-focus sentences have both a doer pronoun and a subject pronoun, sitting right next to each other — which is exactly the condition that triggers fusion.

That is why fused pronouns are so common in Kapampangan: the focus system manufactures the collisions, and fusion resolves them. The two features exist because of each other.

4. The marker swap

The whole mechanism in one table:

 Actor focusObject focus
The doer gets…ingning
The thing acted on gets…-ng (attached)ing
The verb takes…-um-, mag-, maN-i-, -an
ExampleSumulat yang poesia ing lalakiIsulat ne ning lalaki ing poesia

ing is the spotlight. Whichever noun the verb nominates, the spotlight moves there and the other noun steps back to ning.

5. Choosing a focus

Which one should you use? In practice, focus tracks what is already under discussion — much like English choosing between "a poem" and "the poem".

  • Talking about the boy and what he is up to? Actor focus.
  • Talking about the poem and what happens to it? Object focus.
  • The thing acted on is specific and known? Object focus is likelier.

As a learner, you can survive on actor focus alone for quite a while. But you cannot understand Kapampangan without object focus, because speakers use it constantly.

🌱 The honest state of play

Focus is the single concept that separates people who "did some Kapampangan" from people who can use it. It takes months, not days, and it will feel arbitrary long after you can describe it correctly.

That gap between understanding a rule and using it is not a sign you have failed to learn it. It is what learning it looks like from the inside.

6. There is more where this came from

For completeness, and so nothing surprises you later: Kapampangan has five voices, not two.

VoicePuts in focusLevel
Agent (actor)the doerthis lesson
Patient (object)the thing acted onthis lesson
Goalthe recipientB1
Locativethe placeB1
Circumstantialthe instrument or beneficiaryB1

An example of what waits: Panyulat neng poesia ning lalaki ing pen — "the boy will write a poem with the pen", with the pen itself in focus. That is the circumstantial voice, and it is genuinely beyond A2.

You are not missing anything by stopping at two. Actor and object focus cover the overwhelming majority of everyday sentences.

7. Practice

Exercise 1: Which marker on the doer?

  • Object focus → the doer takes ___ (Answer: ning)

  • "I wrote to him." → "Silatanan ___." (Answer: ke)

  • The verb takes -um-, mag- or maN-. → ___ focus (Answer: actor)

  • "saw" → ___ (Answer: ikit)
  • "called" → ___ (Answer: minaus)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Is object focus just the passive? No. It carries none of the passive's markedness. Using object focus in Kapampangan is as ordinary as using the active voice in English.

How do I know whether a verb takes i- or -an? Partly it depends on the role in focus — -an tends toward recipients and locations, i- toward things moved or transferred. But there is genuine lexical arbitrariness, and native speakers learn it verb by verb.

Can I just avoid object focus? When speaking, mostly yes, for now. When listening, absolutely not.

Why This Lesson Matters

Focus is the organising principle of Kapampangan grammar and of Philippine languages generally. Every marker in lesson 3, every fused pronoun in lesson 5 and every affix in lesson 11 exists to serve it. Understanding focus is the point at which the language stops being a pile of rules and becomes a system.
