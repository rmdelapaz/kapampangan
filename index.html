20 Lessons — a Complete A1–A2 Foundation

Kapampangan is spoken by around two million people in Pampanga, Tarlac, Bataan and parts of Nueva Ecija and Bulacan. It is not a dialect of Tagalog — it belongs to the Central Luzon branch, and its closest relative is Sambal.

This course takes you from your first greeting to the point where you can hold a market transaction, describe your family, negate, question, and read an object-focus sentence without flinching. It covers the CEFR A1 and A2 levels in full.

Why twenty lessons and not sixteen? Because Kapampangan needs three lessons where most courses need one. Its marker series, its two pronoun sets, and above all its fused pronouns — ke, me, ne, da ka — have no Tagalog equivalent, and a learner who skips them cannot parse ordinary speech. Lessons 3, 4 and 5 are where the extra room goes.

“Is Kapampangan a dialect?”

No — and the question is worth answering properly, because the word "dialect" has done real damage to Philippine regional languages.

Kapampangan is a separate language. In the Austronesian family it sits in the Central Luzon group, whose other members include Sambal and Bolinao. Tagalog is in a different branch entirely. The two are not parent and child, and they are not close cousins.

The clearest proof is in lesson 11. The written form susulat exists in both languages: in Kapampangan it means is writing, in Tagalog it means will write. Sumulat means will write in Kapampangan and wrote in Tagalog. Shared vocabulary with divergent grammar is what separate languages look like, not what dialects look like.

Calling it a dialect implies it is a regional flavour of the national language and that speaking it is a lesser form of speaking Filipino. It is neither. Kapampangans call it Amanung Sisuan — the language suckled from birth — and lesson 20 explains why that phrase carries the weight it does.

A word on the audio. No browser ships a Kapampangan text-to-speech voice, so the 🔊 buttons use a Filipino (Tagalog) voice as a stand-in. It gets the vowels and most consonants right and the stress frequently wrong. Treat it as a rough guide rather than a model — and if your device has no Filipino voice installed, the audio layer hides itself rather than reading Kapampangan in an English accent.
