1. What it is

Bergaño's Vocabulario de la Lengua Pampanga en Romance is the first Kapampangan dictionary. It circulates in an English translation, which makes it far more usable than the Arte for most readers.

It is not a wordlist. Entries carry the accent class, the part of speech, the derived forms, the passives the root admits, and often an example sentence or a note on where a usage is current.

2. Anatomy of an entry

A real one, reproduced as it stands:

ASBOC. (pp.) Noun, the mouth, including the mouth of a jar or pitcher. Mayasboc, talkative, morally, the gossiper, magatal a asboc…

PartWhat it tells you
ASBOCthe headword, capitals, old orthography
(pp.)the accent class — penultimate
Nounpart of speech
the mouth…the gloss, with its range: a person's mouth and a jar's
Mayasboca derived form, with its own gloss
magatal a asboca collocation

Another, showing two senses under one shape:

PUSU. (g.) Noun, the heart of living things, and of various fruits: verb, when such fruits come out, or appear in the form of a heart. Pusuan, the plant that has heart-shaped fruits, like the coconut palm, the banana, etc.

PUSU. (a., g.) Noun, the groin, belly. Mapusú, adjective, one with a protruding, prominent…

Two headwords, same letters, different accent classes — (g.) against (a., g.) — and different meanings. This is the six-way contrast of lesson 10 in miniature, and it is why the accent marks matter.

3. His abbreviations

(g.)guttural

P. 1.first passive — the i- passive

So an entry reading "Active verb and its infinitive… P. 1.… Mi, frequentative" is telling you the full derivational profile of the root: which passives it admits, which particles it takes, and whether it has a frequentative.

That profile is exactly what lesson 3 said you cannot predict. The dictionary is where the unpredictable part lives — which is why Bergaño kept telling his reader to go there.

4. Searching it

⚠️ Convert before you search, or you will get a false negative

This is not a hypothetical. During this course's A1–A2 audit, five everyday words were listed as unattested. Four of them were in the dictionary all along:

SearchedFoundActually spelled
asbuknothingASBOC
katawannothingcatauan
pusufoundPUSU
pisalifoundpisali
dakenothinggenuinely absent

The conversion rules are lesson 2's. Apply them before concluding a word is not there, and apply them in several variants — a word may have both a c and a u to convert.

A working procedure:

  1. Convert k → c before a/o/u, k → qu before e/i.
  2. Convert w → u.
  3. Try final u as o, and final o as u.
  4. Search for the root, not a derived form.
  5. If still nothing, search the English gloss instead.

Step five is what confirmed asbuk: searching the English for "the mouth" turned up ASBOC as a headword immediately.

5. What a dictionary settles

🌱 Attestation is not currency

Finding asbuk in Bergaño proves it was a Kapampangan word for "mouth" in 1736. It does not prove anyone says it now — though in this case Forman's body parts and ordinary usage both support it.

The reverse matters more. dake is absent from Bergaño and from Forman, which is decent evidence against it — but absence from a dictionary is weaker than presence in one. A 300-year-old book not containing a word is not proof the word does not exist.

This course removed dake on that reasoning. That is a judgement call, and a speaker could overturn it tomorrow.

6. Practice

Exercise 1: Read an entry

From ASBOC. (pp.) Noun, the mouth…

  • The accent class is ___ (Answer: pp)
  • The part of speech is ___ (Answer: noun|Noun)
  • The derived form given is ___ (Answer: mayasboc)
  • In modern spelling the headword is ___ (Answer: asbuk)

Exercise 2: His abbreviations

  • P. 1. means the first ___ (Answer: passive)
  • (g.) means ___ (Answer: guttural)
  • (a.) or (pc.) means the accent is on the ___ syllable (Answer: final|last)

Exercise 3: Convert before searching

  • To find katawan, search ___ (Answer: catauan)
  • To find keti, search ___ (Answer: queti)
  • To find asbuk, search ___ (Answer: asboc)

Exercise 4: What it proves

Answer yes or no.

  • An entry proves the word was attested in the 1730s. ___ (Answer: yes)
  • An entry proves the word is used today. ___ (Answer: no)
  • Absence proves a word does not exist. ___ (Answer: no)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Is there a modern Kapampangan dictionary? There are modern wordlists and community projects, but nothing with Bergaño's derivational detail is freely available. That is the gap lesson 14 returns to.

Should I trust a 1736 gloss? For what the word meant then, yes. For now, treat it as a strong hypothesis to check.

Why does he give accent classes when modern writing drops them? Because accent is phonemic. Modern orthography loses information the dictionary preserves — one of the few places where the old system is straightforwardly better.

Why This Lesson Matters

The dictionary is where every unpredictable fact about a root lives, and both grammars keep pointing at it. Being able to use it is the difference between following a course and doing your own work.
