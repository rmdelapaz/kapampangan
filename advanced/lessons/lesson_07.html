1. Ten years on one prefix

Bergaño opens his chapter on maca- with an unusual confession:

"Otros le llaman de consumar; yo le pudiera llamar de consumir, porque me ha consumido mucho tiempo, pues hace cerca de diez años que ando trabajando en él."

Others call it the prefix of consummating; I could call it the prefix of consuming, because it has consumed a great deal of my time — I have been working on it for close to ten years.

He calls it "la composición más ambigua y difícil de entender que hay en toda la lengua" — the most ambiguous and difficult composition in the whole language — "because being one, it admits many and various significations with the very same verbs."

2. One form, four readings

His demonstration. A single sentence:

Mekasulat ku.

1I have written
2I have finished writing
3I wrote by mistake
4I was able to write

— and five, he adds, counting the maca of misgiving.

Four unrelated English sentences from one Kapampangan form, disambiguated only by context. This is not a learner's confusion; it is a property of the language, recorded by someone who worked on it for a decade.

3. The six sections

SectionSenseRough gloss
maca de perfeccióncompletionhas done, has finished
maca potencialabilitycan, is able to
maca de engorronuisanceis a bother to
maca causalcausationcauses, brings about
maca / paca de estarstateis in the condition of
maca / paca de intensiónintensityis very

Bergaño is careful about the first: maca de perfección is misunderstood, he says, if taken to mean only a perfect and consummated action. That reading is "tomar el rábano por las hojas" — taking the radish by its leaves — and makes the composition not merely harder but impossible to understand.

His own account: it does not say doing, but says what the agent is supposed to do by exercising the verb, and it always speaks as of something past.

💡 Where Forman's maka- fits

Forman's examples — makatamad "makes one lazy", makaputla "makes you pale", makasalikut "hidden" — are Bergaño's causal, de estar and de intensión senses.

So Forman is describing three of Bergaño's six, accurately. He simply does not cover the other three, and does not claim to.

4. The potential

Bergaño's section on maca potencial is explicit:

Makasulat ku. — I can write.

Makasulat ku pota or bukas. — I will be able to write later, or tomorrow.

Mekasulat ku. — I was able to write.

And in the passive, with the a- forms the B1 tier taught:

Atalastas ku iyan. — I can understand that.

Atalastas ku pota. — I will be able to understand it later.

Atalastas ku na. — I was already able to understand it.

He notes that in the present, this sense "sólo dice potencia y no acto" — expresses only potency, not act — which is why the ordinary present of the verb does not correspond to it.

It also has a habitual future: makasulat ya, "he has the habit of being able to write." And frequentatives for things involving strength: abuat-buat ku iyan, "I can lift that repeatedly."

5. Proximate and remote

Here is the distinction worth carrying away. sukat is also a potential — Bergaño calls it "el más adecuado a nuestro romance puedo", the closest to our word "I can."

FormCovers
maca-potencia próxima y expedita — ability that is near and unobstructed
sukatpotencia próxima y remota — both near and remote ability

His two examples make it concrete.

Of someone who does not know how to write, you cannot say he "could" with maca-, because that is remote potency. With sukat you can: sukat yang sumulat.

And of a man in shackles:
Sukat yang lumakad, dapot ali ya makalakad.
He is able to walk, but he cannot walk.

That sentence is only a contradiction in English. In Kapampangan it says: walking is within his powers, but not available to him now. English needs a paraphrase; Kapampangan has two words.

Beginner lesson 16 gave you sukat as "ought to, fitting." That is a different use of the same word — and a reminder that the beginner glosses are starting points, not definitions.

6. What this course got wrong, twice

🌱 A worked example of how not to reason

First error. B1 lesson 8 was drafted as "the potentive maka-," straight from Tagalog, where maka- is the potentive. No Kapampangan source was consulted. That is reasoning from a related language.

Second error. Reading Forman showed his maka- examples are all statives, so the lesson was rewritten to state flatly that maka- is not the potentive, with a warning that assuming otherwise would mislead. That is reasoning from one source's silence.

What Bergaño shows. Both the stative and the potential are real. The prefix is polysemous, with at least six senses. The confident correction was as wrong as the thing it corrected, and wrong in a more irritating way, because it was delivered as a caution against other people's errors.

The two mistakes share a shape: deciding a question from the material in front of me rather than from the material that exists. B1 lesson 8 now says so, and so does this one.

7. Practice

Exercise 1: The four readings

Give four English readings of Mekasulat ku. One word each.

  • I have ___ (Answer: written)
  • I have ___ writing (Answer: finished)
  • I wrote by ___ (Answer: mistake|error)
  • I was ___ to write (Answer: able)

Exercise 2: The senses

  • Bergaño gives ___ sections on maca- (Answer: six|6)
  • Forman's makatamad belongs to which of them? maca ___ (Answer: causal)
  • makasulat ku means I ___ write (Answer: can)

Exercise 3: Proximate and remote

  • maca- covers only ___ ability (Answer: proximate|near)
  • sukat covers proximate and ___ (Answer: remote)
  • Of a man in shackles: "Sukat yang lumakad, dapot ali ya ___." (Answer: makalakad)

Exercise 4: The a- passive

  • "I can understand that." → "___ ku iyan." (Answer: atalastas)
  • "I can lift that repeatedly." → "___ ku iyan." (Answer: abuat-buat)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

How do I know which sense is meant? Context, and only context. Bergaño could not give a rule after ten years; you will not find one either.

Should I use maca- for ability? You can, for proximate ability. For remote ability use sukat; for permission, malyari.

Has it narrowed since 1736? Possibly. Forman's silence on three senses is weak evidence, but it is evidence. A speaker could settle it in a minute.

Why This Lesson Matters

maca- is common, ambiguous, and the single most likely place for a confident learner to construct a sentence that means something they did not intend.
