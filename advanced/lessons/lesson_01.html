1. Why this tier exists

At the end of the B1–B2 tier, lesson 22 answered the question "is there a C1 tier?" with: "Not for Kapampangan, and building one responsibly would need sources this course does not have."

That was true when it was written. Then the sources turned up.

Diego Bergaño, Arte de la Lengua Pampanga (1736) and his Vocabulario de la Lengua Pampanga — the first grammar and the first dictionary of Kapampangan — are both in the public domain and both online.

This tier is built on them. It is not "advanced conversation"; for a language with this documentary record, C1 and C2 realistically mean reading the tradition and handling the sources.

2. Who Bergaño was

An Augustinian friar, 1690–1747. Examiner for the Archbishopric of Manila. And — the detail that matters for this course — Prior of the Convento de Bacolor.

💡 Two grammars, one town, 235 years apart

Bacolor is where Forman did his fieldwork in 1968–69, and he chose it for its reputation as the "Athens of Pampanga." He notes that the two most substantial earlier studies — Bergaño's and Parker's — were done there too.

So the two grammars this course rests on describe the same community's speech, separated by two and a third centuries. That is an unusual thing to have for any language, and it is what makes the historical comparisons in this tier possible.

Bergaño is not a detached observer. He was a colonial missionary writing so that other missionaries could preach. That shapes what he records and what he thinks is worth recording — and it is worth keeping in view.

3. His terms

He describes Kapampangan in the vocabulary of Latin grammar, because that was the available toolkit.

"Declension" is the most misleading. Bergaño knows it: he says the nouns of this language are invariables en la voz — they never change shape — and that what varies is the article placed before them. He is describing ing, ning and king with a borrowed word, and he says so.

⚠️ This course got caught by exactly this

During the A1–A2 audit, five everyday words were flagged as unattested. Searching Bergaño's dictionary for asbuk returned nothing — because he spells it ASBOC, and there it is as a headword: "the mouth, including the mouth of a jar or pitcher."

Four of the five were attested all along. The search was wrong, not the vocabulary. Apply the substitutions before concluding a word is not there.

5. What he has that Forman does not

BergañoForman
A chapter on protocompuestos with full assimilation rulesA page of assimilation examples
Six sections on maca-A handful of stative examples
Chapters on mag-, mi-, maqui-, ma-, ca-, pa-Scattered mentions
Ten sections of adverbs, including ironyOne section of lexical particles
Ordinals, distributives, money-countingCardinals only
Six-way accent contrastsOne minimal pair

Against which, Forman has a century and a half of linguistics behind him, describes the living language of his own time, and does not force Kapampangan into Latin categories. Neither replaces the other.

6. How to read a 1736 grammar

🌱 Three cautions

It is not current. Bergaño describes the language of the 1730s. Some of what he records was already archaic by Forman's time. Where they disagree, that may be change rather than error.

It is not neutral. He is a missionary writing for missionaries. His examples run heavily to sin, obligation and the sacraments.

It is not a modern description. "Passive" is his best available word for something that is not really a passive. Read through the terminology to the data.

None of that makes him unreliable. He spent years on this language and says plainly when he is unsure — of maca- he writes that it has consumed him for close to ten years and is "the most ambiguous and difficult composition in the whole language." That is a scholar you can trust.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do I need Spanish? For the Arte, yes — or patience with a translation tool. The Vocabulario exists in English, which is why lessons 9 and 10 lean on it.

Is a 1736 grammar really the best available? For derivational morphology, on the evidence, yes. That is a statement about how little has been published since, not about how good Bergaño is — though he is good.

Should I trust him over Forman? Neither, by default. Lesson 14 returns to this.

Why This Lesson Matters

Everything else in this tier assumes you can open these two books and work. That is the actual C1 skill for Kapampangan: not fluency drills, but the ability to consult the primary record and judge it.
