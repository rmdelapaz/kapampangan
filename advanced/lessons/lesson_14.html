1. What the sources say they do not know

Both grammarians mark their own limits, and it is worth collecting them in one place. This is the best-documented map of Kapampangan available, and it has holes its authors point at.

SourceOpen question
FormanThe variation between keta, ken and kyan "is not yet well understood."
Forman"Considerable lexical-semantic-syntactic analysis remains to be done on the classification of verb roots."
FormanWhether pi- forms are verbs or nouns: "it is not clear."
FormanDialect surveying "remains a task to be undertaken."
FormanThe claim that the Japanese occupation was a turning point "merits further investigation."
Bergañomaca- is "the most ambiguous and difficult composition in the whole language" — after ten years.
BergañoWhich roots admit syncope: no rule, beyond one negative case.
BergañoOne accent type has no equivalent in Spanish or Latin and is "difficult to pronounce, if not impossible."
BergañoPreterites of plain disyllables: "no remedy but to examine them one by one."

🌱 Nine admissions of ignorance is a good sign

A source that never says "I do not know" is either describing something very simple or overclaiming. Both of these men worked for years and marked the edges of what they had established.

When you meet a confident, unhedged account of Kapampangan grammar, check whether its confidence is earned or inherited.

2. Where this course was wrong

Not a disclaimer — a worked case study, since the whole tier has been about judging sources.

ClaimWhat happened
"Kapampangan has tones"Inherited from an earlier version of this site. False. Stress is phonemic; tone is not a feature.
"The schwa is unique to Kapampangan"Backwards. Ilocano and Pangasinan have schwa phonemes.
"Sinulog is a Kapampangan festival"It is Cebu's.
"obakit, not bakit"Invented. Forman lists obat, ot and bakit.
ati covers "have"Incomplete. atin is the HAVE form.
Fused pronouns are "avoided in questions"Wrong. There is a second series for interrogatives.
The deictics, twiceTaught one way, switched to Forman, switched back on Bergaño's evidence.
"maka- is not the potentive"An over-correction. It is polysemous.
"dake" as vocabularyUnattested in both sources. Removed.

⚠️ The two failure modes

Reasoning from Tagalog. The maka- draft, and the instinct behind several others. Shared vocabulary makes the divergences more dangerous, not less.

Treating one source's silence as evidence. The maka- correction, and the first deictic switch. A grammar not mentioning something is not a grammar denying it.

Both are the same error underneath: deciding a question from the material in front of you rather than from the material that exists.

3. What is missing from the record

MissingWhy it matters
A modern reference grammarThe most recent is 1971, from six months in one town.
A dialect surveyForman called for one and it is still wanting. The eastern king and the western -ay → -e are all we have.
A modern dictionary with derivational detailBergaño is 1736. Nothing freely available matches its depth.
An accessible literary anthologyWhy this tier has no lessons on poetics or close reading — there were no primary texts to work from.
A corpusNo way to ask how often anything is actually said.

That list is why this tier stops at fourteen lessons rather than running to eighteen. The material for the others does not exist in reachable form, and inventing it would have been the easiest thing in the world.

4. Two grammars, one town

Step back and look at what this course rests on.

Bergaño — Augustinian friar, Prior of the Convento de Bacolor, publishing in 1736.

Forman — linguist, fieldwork in Bacolor, barrio Cabalantian, October 1968 to April 1969.

Two hundred and thirty-five years apart, in the same town. That is an extraordinary thing to have, and it is also a sharp limitation: what you have learned is Bacolor Kapampangan, at two moments.

Forman chose Bacolor for its reputation as the "Athens of Pampanga," the home of the best-known Kapampangan writers. Prestige varieties are not neutral. The speech of a literary town is not the speech of the province.

💡 What changed, and what did not

Comparing the two is one of the few things a Kapampangan learner can do that almost nobody has done.

Stable across 235 years: the marker system, the pronoun sets, the fused pronouns, the a→e and u→i changes, the maN- assimilations, phonemic stress.

Apparently changed or narrowed: the six senses of maca-, the anticipatory meca- counting, possibly the deictic assignment.

Every one of those is a hypothesis, not a finding. Checking them is real work that is genuinely available to you now.

🌱 The closing note

You began with Mayap a abak and you end able to read an eighteenth-century grammar, weigh it against a twentieth-century one, and say which you believe and why.

Along the way this course told you at least nine things that were wrong, and corrected each when a better source turned up. That is not a flaw in the method; it is the method. The alternative — never revising — would have left every one of those errors in place.

So take nothing here as settled, including this. Check it against a speaker. If you find something wrong, you will be in a very small group of people who could have found it, and the language will be slightly better documented for it.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Should I trust this course? Less than you trust a speaker, more than you trust an unsourced page. Every substantive claim here names where it came from, which is what makes it checkable.

Is there a C2+ tier? Not from documents. What follows is original work: fieldwork, reading literature, or talking to people.

What is the single most useful thing I can do now? Find a speaker and stop switching to English when it gets hard.
