1. Six words spelled sala

Beginner lesson 1 taught that stress is phonemic and gave one pair. Bergaño gives this:

AccentMeans
neither guttural nor acuteguilt, sin
(pp) guttural, not acutelight, brightness — as of the sun
acute, not gutturalwoven cane, like a grill
acute, not gutturalto fault, as a defect
(pp) neither guttural nor acutea physical error; to miss, to err
acute and gutturalfor a woman to conceive

Six meanings, one spelling, distinguished by two independent features — where the accent falls, and whether the syllable is guttural.

He introduces the list with a warning: "Va mucho en distinguir estos acentos, porque si no, dirá uno muchas veces pan por peras" — a great deal depends on distinguishing these accents, because otherwise you will often say "bread" when you mean "pears."

2. More sets

WordSenses Bergaño lists
lasaflavour · pain or torment · cane for arrows · an adverb meaning "almost, hardly"
balaone who has dominion over a thing · to understand wrongly · to ask in advance against the harvest · to threaten in words
babaa load carried on the back · to go down, to humble oneself

Lasa is the one to sit with. You met it in B1 lesson 4 as the root behind lasawan, "to melt or digest." Bergaño gives it four unrelated senses, one of which is an adverb. Whatever you thought lasa meant, it means more.

💡 Why the dictionary is organised by accent

Now the (pp.), (a.) and (g.) marks from lesson 9 look different. They are not pronunciation notes appended for completeness — they are what distinguishes the headwords.

Two entries both reading PUSU, one marked (g.) and one (a., g.), are two different words. Drop the marks and the dictionary stops working.

3. How specific a root can be

Browsing the Vocabulario teaches something no wordlist can: Kapampangan roots are often far more specific than any English word.

RootGloss
bugusscarred from little itches — "in Candava there are many who have these scars"
angabto be with the mouth open, like a birdling being fed by the parent bird, or an infant waiting to suck
alualto clean the mouth with the tongue, as when food remains between the molars
aguiapa blister or crack at the corner of the mouth
buyagyagcrumbled — also said of cooked rice when taken from the pot
abauthe food left over

Angab is the one that stays with you. A single root for the posture of an open mouth waiting to be fed, with both the birdling and the infant given as the typical cases. English needs a clause.

Note also the geographic note under bugus — Bergaño records where a thing is common. He is documenting a society as well as a language.

4. Near-synonyms in English, distinct roots here

The practical consequence: an English word will often correspond to several Kapampangan roots that are not interchangeable.

Search the Vocabulario for "mouth" and you get ASBOC (the mouth, including a jar's), ALUAL (to clean the mouth with the tongue), ANGAB (to hold the mouth open), AGUIAP (a crack at the corner of the mouth), AL-LUA (the mouth of a river), LIUD (itching in the mouth) — each a separate root.

🌱 The trap this closes

A learner working from a bilingual wordlist builds a mental model in which each English word has one Kapampangan equivalent. That model is wrong in both directions: one Kapampangan form may cover six senses, and one English word may correspond to six roots.

Working from a dictionary with full entries is the cure. It is slower, and it is the reason C2 exists.

5. Building vocabulary from the source

A method for going beyond any course's wordlist:

  1. Pick a semantic field — cooking, weather, illness, fishing.
  2. Search the English glosses, not the headwords. You do not know the Kapampangan yet.
  3. Collect every root that comes back, with its accent class.
  4. Note the derived forms each entry gives — those are free vocabulary.
  5. Check against Forman where he overlaps, and flag anything only Bergaño has as possibly archaic.
  6. Check with a speaker before using it.

The words in this tier's review deck were built exactly this way. Where a gloss reads oddly, that is Bergaño's translator, preserved rather than smoothed — you are seeing the source, not a paraphrase of it.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do modern speakers keep all six senses of sala? Almost certainly not all. Accent distinctions are exactly what erodes under pressure from another language.

How do I learn the guttural quality? From a speaker. Bergaño says it has no equivalent in Spanish or Latin and is hard to pronounce if not impossible.

Is this level of specificity unusual? No — most languages are this specific in the fields their speakers live in. It only looks unusual because learner materials flatten it.

Why This Lesson Matters

This is where a learner stops treating Kapampangan as English with different words. Six meanings under one spelling, and six roots under one English gloss, are the same fact seen from two sides: the two languages carve the world differently.
