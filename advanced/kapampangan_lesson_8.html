1. pa- and paca-

B1 lesson 9 gave you pa- as the causative core, and said openly that Forman devotes only a few lines to it. Bergaño devotes a chapter, with sections on "the recomposition of facere, pacere, repacere" and on "otras significaciones de el pa" — other meanings of pa.

The Latin joke is his: facere is to do, so pacere would be to cause to do, and repacere to cause to cause to do. Kapampangan will stack causatives, and he is reaching for a way to name the stack.

Note how pa-, pi- and pan- form a set: each is the passive-side counterpart of an active particle — ma-/pa-, mi-/pi-, man-/pan-. That symmetry is the tidiest thing in Kapampangan derivational morphology, and it is easier to see in Bergaño's layout than in a modern one.

You have already used several without noticing: paquigagan mu ya in lesson 6, pipingan, panyulat.

2. maguin — to become

maguin (modern maging) heads Bergaño's chapter on various particles. It expresses becoming — turning into a state or a role.

This fills a real gap in the earlier tiers. The beginner course taught existence with ati/atin, identity with bare juxtaposition, and description with ma- — but nothing for entering a state.

Maguin mestru ya. — He becomes a teacher.

Maguin masanting ya. — It becomes beautiful.

Compare the three:

Mestru ya.He is a teacher. (identificational)
Ati ya keni.He is here. (existential)
Maguin mestru ya.He becomes a teacher.

3. si and pasi

Bergaño records two particles, si and pasi, which are little described anywhere else. They sit in his chapter of miscellaneous particles, alongside maguin.

⚠️ The honest position on these

This course is not going to teach you si and pasi, because doing it properly means reading Bergaño's chapter in Spanish and checking the result against a speaker — and neither has been done.

What this lesson does is tell you they exist and where to find them. That is the appropriate service at this level: not every gap should be filled with a confident paragraph, and a course that fills them all is lying somewhere.

If you pursue them, you are doing something genuinely unusual — almost nothing accessible in English describes these.

4. His comparatives

Bergaño gives comparatives their own chapter. B1 lesson 12 covered the modern picture: mising- for equality, the descriptive plus king (optionally with mas and kesa) for inequality, peka- for the superlative.

Lesson 6 added lalo, which he uses for comparisons of excess with the ma- of abundance. Putting them together:

🌱 What C1 actually means here

For a language with a national literature and a dozen modern grammars, C1 means reading novels and following debates.

For Kapampangan it means something narrower and, in its way, more demanding: being able to open the primary record, navigate it, and judge it. You are now in a position where a claim about Kapampangan grammar can be checked rather than believed — including the claims on this site.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why not just teach si and pasi? Because doing it honestly needs work that has not been done. Saying so is more useful than a confident paragraph that might be wrong — as lessons 7 and B1's lesson 8 between them demonstrate.

Is maguin common? Yes, and its modern form maging is shared with Tagalog. One of the safer cognates.

How much of Bergaño does this tier cover? Roughly half, and the half chosen for being checkable and useful. The rest is waiting.
