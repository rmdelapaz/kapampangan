What C1 means for this language

For a language with a national literature and a shelf of modern grammars, C1 means reading novels and following arguments.

For Kapampangan it means something narrower and, in its way, harder: being able to open the primary record, navigate it, and judge it. By the end of this tier a claim about Kapampangan grammar is something you can check rather than believe — including the claims on this site.

Why 14 lessons and not 18? Because that is what the sources support. C1 is eight lessons of deep morphology from Bergaño's Arte; C2 is six on the lexicon, register and craft. There are no lessons on poetics or close literary reading — that needs an anthology of primary texts, and none was in hand. Inventing those lessons would have been the easiest thing in the world and would have made the whole tier untrustworthy.

What this tier is built on

Diego Bergaño, Arte de la Lengua Pampanga (1736) and his Vocabulario de la Lengua Pampanga — the first grammar and the first dictionary of Kapampangan, both public domain. Alongside Michael Forman's Kapampangan Grammar Notes (1971), which carried the B1–B2 tier.

Bergaño was Prior of the Convento de Bacolor. Forman did his fieldwork in Bacolor. The two describe the same community's speech, 235 years apart — which is what makes the historical comparisons in this tier possible, and also fixes its limit: what you learn here is Bacolor Kapampangan, at two moments.

This tier has corrected the rest of the course twice. Reading Bergaño reversed the deictic decision in beginner lesson 6, and showed that B1 lesson 8's confident "maka- is not the potentive" was an overstatement. Lesson 14 collects every claim this course has had to withdraw, because judging sources is the skill the tier is actually teaching.

A word on the audio. No browser ships a Kapampangan voice, so the 🔊 buttons use a Filipino (Tagalog) voice as a stand-in. At this level the warning matters more than before: it will not reproduce the guttural accent Bergaño says has no Spanish or Latin equivalent, and it ignores the stress distinctions that carry meaning throughout lessons 2 and 10.
