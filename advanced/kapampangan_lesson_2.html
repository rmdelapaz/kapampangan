1. The substitutions

B2 lesson 19 gave the outline. Here is the working set, in both directions.

OldModernConditionExample
ckbefore a, o, ucatauan → katawan
qukbefore e, iqueni → keni
ggbefore a, o, ugamat → gamat
gugbefore e, iguinu → ginu
uwbetween vowelscatauan → katawan
ouoften, in final syllablesasboc → asbuk
ñyny mañyulat → manyulat
ñgng Capampañgan → Kapampangan

The direction that matters most is modern → old, because that is the one you need when searching. A word you know in modern spelling will not be found in Bergaño unless you convert it first.

asbuk → k before nothing, final u → search asboc  ✔

katawan → k before a → c; w between vowels → u → search catauan  ✔

keti → k before e → qu → search queti  ✔

2. Where the syllable breaks

Bergaño opens his final chapter with something no modern description states as plainly: a consonant in the middle of a word often does not begin the next syllable. It closes the one before, and the next syllable starts fresh.

caladua — said calad-dua

lagpac — said lag-pac

sacsi — said sac-si

agcas — said ag-cas

He calls these cortadillos — little cuts. And he warns that getting them wrong can change the word:

SaidMeans
quic-cuanthe aborted fetus
guicuanone whose nails are cut by another

Same letters, different cut, unrelated meanings. Bergaño's comment: "va en esto mucho, porque a no hacerlo así, quizás haría mal sentido, o se mudaría la significación" — a great deal turns on this, because otherwise you may make no sense, or change the meaning.

💡 This is what B1 lesson 5's geminates were about

Forman's rule that dakal + la is pronounced daka·la — one consonant, lengthened vowel — is the same phenomenon at a word boundary. Bergaño documents it inside words as well.

3. Length changes meaning

Beginner lesson 1 taught that stress is phonemic, with one minimal pair. Bergaño gives a great many, and distinguishes length as well as position.

Word(pp)(agudo)
lupafacea certain itchy herb
sulatwrittencrazy
bacalironprovisions for a journey
gayacto drag one's clothesarrangement, disposition

Súlat "written" against sulát "crazy" is worth memorising as the example you cite when someone tells you stress does not matter much.

4. Bergaño's accent marks

He uses two marks throughout the Vocabulario, and you need them to read an entry.

MarkNameWhat it means
(agudo)acute or shortstress on the final syllable
(pp)the other classstress on the penultimate

But each of those subdivides, and here Bergaño is unusually candid. The (pp) class has one variety like Spanish, and another that is "agudo y gutural en la última dejándose caer" — for which, he says, there is nothing comparable in Latin or Spanish.

Of the acute guttural variety he writes: "al cual no hay semejante en romance ni en latín; y así, es difícil de pronunciar, si no es imposible." Difficult to pronounce, if not impossible.

🌱 A grammarian admitting defeat

That is a man who spent years on the language telling you he cannot describe one of its sounds in the terms available to him, and doubts he can produce it.

Take it as licence. If a distinction will not come, you are not necessarily failing; you may be at the edge of what the written record can transmit. Sounds travel through speakers, not through books.

Words of three syllables, he adds, often carry a further accent before the penultimate: talicala, talinduay, salimpapao, talipapa.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Is the old orthography worse? It is Spanish-shaped rather than Kapampangan-shaped, so it needs rules the modern one does not. But it marks accent, which the modern one abandons — and accent is phonemic. Each loses something.

Should I mark accents when I write? Not in ordinary modern writing. In study notes, yes — you will thank yourself.

How do I learn the guttural accent? From a speaker. Bergaño could not write it down and neither can this page.

Why This Lesson Matters

Three substitution rules open the entire written record: Bergaño, the prayer books, the periodicals of the 1920s, the signage, the family documents. That is a very large return on a small amount of memorisation.
