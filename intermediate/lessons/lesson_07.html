1. Not past — just past

Beginner lesson 11 gave you three aspects: contemplative, progressive, completed. Kapampangan has a fourth thing it can mark, and it is not on that scale.

The recent-completion predication says an action finished a moment ago. English needs an adverb — "just," "only just," "right now." A Kapampangan verb can carry it in its shape.

2. ka- plus doubling

The construction is ka- plus some form of doubling or reduplication. And unlike the aspectual reduplication of beginner lesson 11 — which copies only the first syllable — this generally doubles the whole root.

ka- + root + root  →  "just finished ...ing"

Karatangratang mi pa. — We just arrived.

I Rita, kapanganakpanganak na pa. — Rita just gave birth.

I Ima, kapuntapunta na pa Menila. — Mother just went to Manila.

Kaketket na pa ning asu. — He was just bitten by a dog.

Kabilibili ku keng libru ku, kinua ne. — I had just put down my book and he took it.

Ding anak, kapagapagaral da pang lisyun. — The children just finished studying the lesson.

Katigtigtigtig na pang masanting a kanta ing radio.
The radio just finished playing a nice song.

That last one doubles tigtig — itself already a doubled-looking root — into tigtigtigtig. Kapampangan is entirely comfortable with this.

3. The particle pa

Look back at those examples and count the pas. Almost every one has it: mi pa, na pa, da pang, ku pang.

You met pa in the beginner course as "still, yet" — the thing that makes ali pa mean "not yet." Here it reinforces the recency: the event is still, as it were, warm.

💡 pa and na are a pair

na — now, already, by this point
pa — still, yet, as of now

Between them they carry an enormous amount of Kapampangan's temporal nuance, and they turn up in almost every sentence of connected speech. Lesson 14 gives the particles a full treatment.

4. The kaibat variant

Some verbs form the construction differently: kaibat — itself doubled to kaibatibat — followed by the verb.

Kaibatibat ku pang mininum. — I just came from drinking.

Kaibatibat na pang migising Mike. — Mike just woke up.

Kaibatibat kung mengan. — I just finished eating.

Kaibatibat ke pang biniru. — I just finished teasing him.

Kaibatibat kung mengan is a nice summary of where you are: mengan is the completed form of mangan from beginner lesson 11, and kaibatibat layers "just now" on top of it.

5. Against ordinary completed aspect

KapampanganEnglishWhat it says
Mengan ku.I ate.completed, no claim about when
Kaibatibat kung mengan.I just finished eating.completed a moment ago
Dintang ya.He arrived.completed
Karatangratang mi pa.We just arrived.completed a moment ago

And remember from beginner lesson 5 that Dintang ne — with the fused pronoun — already shifts "he arrived" toward "he has arrived." Between the aspect system, the fused pronouns and this construction, Kapampangan draws distinctions English needs several extra words for.

🌱 Recognition before production

You will hear this constantly — it is how people answer "have you eaten?" and "when did you get here?" — long before you produce it comfortably.

That is the right order. A construction you can hear is already doing work for you, even if you would still reach for ngeni plus a completed verb to say the same thing.

6. Practice

Exercise 1: Build the form

  • The recent-completion prefix is ___ (Answer: ka-|ka)
  • It is combined with doubling of the whole ___ (Answer: root)
  • The particle that almost always accompanies it is ___ (Answer: pa)

Exercise 2: Find the root

  • karatangratang is built on ___ (Answer: ratang|datang)
  • kapuntapunta is built on ___ (Answer: punta)
  • kabilibili is built on ___ (Answer: bili)
  • kaketket is built on ___ (Answer: ket)

Exercise 3: na or pa?

  • "now, already" is ___ (Answer: na)
  • "still, yet" is ___ (Answer: pa)

Exercise 4: Complete the sentences

  • "I just finished eating." → "Kaibatibat kung ___." (Answer: mengan)
  • "Mike just woke up." → "Kaibatibat na pang ___ Mike." (Answer: migising)
  • "We just arrived." → "___ mi pa." (Answer: karatangratang)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Is this an aspect? Forman treats it as a predication type rather than a fourth aspect. Practically the distinction does not matter much; what matters is that it is a separate thing from the three aspects you know.

Which verbs take kaibat instead? Forman does not give a rule — he presents both patterns and lists examples. Treat it as lexical.

Can I just say "ngeni" instead? You can be understood that way. But "I just ate" and "I ate now" are not the same sentence, and speakers hear the difference.

Why This Lesson Matters

"Just did it" is one of the highest-frequency things anyone says, and Kapampangan gives it dedicated machinery. Recognising the doubled root is also good practice for the reduplication you will meet throughout the rest of this tier.
