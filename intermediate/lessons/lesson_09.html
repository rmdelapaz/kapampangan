1. The causative idea

A causative says you had something done rather than doing it. English manages with "have," "get" and "make": I had my hair cut, she got the car fixed, they made him wait.

Kapampangan puts it on the verb, with pa- at the centre of a small family of affixes.

2. pa- inside the verb

Forman lists pa- plainly as the affix meaning 'cause', sitting alongside the tense and focus affixes on the verb.

Ing papalambat king taba ya ing papasyas king ebun.
What softens the lard hardens the egg.

Patyeya muli, patagal de king asu.
If he doesn't go home, they'll sic the dog on him.

E ne pepakit ing iya, talagang tatakut ya.
He didn't let it show that he was really afraid.

Papalambat and papasyas are worth unpacking: lambat "soft" and syas "hard" are qualities, and pa- turns each into "cause to be that." The lard does not become soft; something makes it soft.

And pepakit — "let it be shown" — is pa- + pakit ("show"), with the a → e completed-aspect change from lesson 4.

3. ipa- — the thing caused in focus

Combine the causative pa- with the focus prefix i- and you get ipa-, which puts the caused thing in focus.

Ipabusbus me balugbug i Julieta.
Have Julieta's ears pierced for her.

Ipanik mu la kang ima mu.
Take them up to your mother.

You met the first of those in lesson 2, filed under beneficiary. It is both: a causative and a beneficiary construction. The affixes stack because the meanings stack.

4. magpa- and magpaka-

magpa- puts the causer in focus — the one who brings the situation about.

Obat la magpalimus? — Why are they begging?

Literally that is closer to "why are they causing alms [to come to them]?" — limus being "alms." Forman lists other roots taking magpa-: lipas, durut, tuyo, gatu, talan, baya.

magpaka- adds a reflexive twist — making oneself become something.

Ot magpakatuling ka? — Why are you trying to darken yourself?

Other magpaka- roots Forman gives: bayani ("hero"), lalaki ("man"), bayat. So magpakabayani is roughly "make a hero of oneself."

💡 Note the ot

Both examples ask "why" — and one uses obat while the other uses ot. These are the short forms the beginner course's question lesson was corrected to include, appearing here in Forman's own examples.

5. The related magka-

Nearby sits magka-, "come to have" or "come to be":

Migkamali ku. — I made a mistake.

Mali is "mistake"; migkamali is "came to have a mistake." Note magka- → migka- in the completed form — the same vowel change as mag- → meg-/mig-.

Forman lists lutu and lub among other magka- roots.

And remember from lesson 13's preview: mika- is one of the ways Kapampangan expresses "have" — Mikaapat ta palang pesus, "to our surprise we have four pesos."

6. How much is actually documented

⚠️ This lesson is thinner than the others, on purpose

Forman's grammar is 100 pages covering the whole language, and causatives get a handful of lines — one example sentence for magpa-, one for magpaka-, one for magka-, plus lists of roots without glosses.

This lesson presents what is attested and stops. It does not fill the gap by importing the Tagalog causative system, because lesson 8 just demonstrated where that leads.

If you need causatives productively, this is a good moment to work with a speaker rather than a book.

What you can safely take away: pa- is the causative core; ipa- focuses the caused thing; magpa- focuses the causer; magpaka- is reflexive; and the causee, when named, is marked with king or kang.

7. Practice

Exercise 1: Which affix?

  • The causative core is ___ (Answer: pa-|pa)
  • The causer is put in focus by ___ (Answer: magpa-|magpa)
  • The caused thing is put in focus by ___ (Answer: ipa-|ipa)
  • Making oneself become something uses ___ (Answer: magpaka-|magpaka)

Exercise 2: Break it down

  • papalambat is pa- plus the root ___ (Answer: lambat)
  • pepakit is pa- plus the root ___ (Answer: pakit)
  • migkamali is magka- plus the root ___ (Answer: mali)
  • magpalimus is magpa- plus the root ___ (Answer: limus)

Exercise 3: Marking the causee

  • A common-noun causee is marked with ___ (Answer: king|keng)
  • A named-person causee is marked with ___ (Answer: kang)

Exercise 4: Complete the sentences

  • "Why are they begging?" → "Obat la ___?" (Answer: magpalimus)
  • "I made a mistake." → "___ ku." (Answer: migkamali)
  • "Take them up to your mother." → "___ mu la kang ima mu." (Answer: ipanik)

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why is this lesson shorter? Because the source is. Padding it with plausible-looking forms would make the course less trustworthy, not more complete.

Can I use pa- freely on any root? No — like every Kapampangan affix, it is lexically restricted. Learn the attested ones first.

Where can I read more? Bergaño's 1736 Arte de la Lengua Pampanga and his Vocabulario are far more detailed on derivation than Forman, and both are in the public domain.

Why This Lesson Matters

Causatives cover a large slice of daily errands — getting your hair cut, having food cooked, sending someone to fetch something. Even at recognition level they stop you from mis-assigning who actually did what.
